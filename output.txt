# Hi

the morning after sunrise
as the city stirs and whispers 
it's a peculiar feeling 
to be greeted with a gentle "hi"

<img src="https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/7/7b/R211_G_trains_at_Smith%E2%80%93Ninth_Streets_September_2025_2.jpg" alt="A Court Square-bound R211 G train at Smith–Ninth Streets station" />

in an era where faces are tracked 
and names are reduced to abbreviations 
a simple nod or a smile is a declaration of ownership 
of this fleeting moment, this instant

<img src="https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/4/40/Tony_Leung_as_the_President_of_the_International_Competition_Jury_at_Opening_Ceremony_of_the_Tokyo_International_Film_Festival_2024_%2854578353950%29.jpg" alt="Tony Leung Chiu-wai" />

we find ourselves lost in the labyrinthine streets
as cars speed past, horns honk 
the cacophony a reminder that we're all just trying to navigate 
this never-ending cycle of getting places and meeting deadlines

<img src="https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/c/cc/Korean_language_greetings.png" alt="The Korean "안녕하세요/안녕하십니까" means "hello." There is a difference in nuance, but it means the same thing. 한국어로 "안녕하세요/안녕하십니까"는 "Hello"라는 뜻입니다. 분위기는 약간 다르지만 뜻은 동일합니다." />

but amidst the chaos, there's a comfort 
a familiarity that's almost palpable 
a warm "hi" that invites us in 
to share stories, secrets, and dreams

it's an all-purpose greeting suitable for most situations, but may not be suitable in formal circumstances
its versatility lies in its universality 
a universal language that transcends borders and backgrounds

think of the countless conversations started or continued with just a few "hilux greetings" 
from strangers who've crossed paths at coffee shops and bookstores 
where the shared word becomes a bridge between cultures and languages

we tend to forget that even in spaces designed for silence 
a voice, any voice, carries weight 
a single hello can be a powerful reminder of humanity's existence

note: as it turns out, the Lakota and Sioux tribes have historical use for words like "hi" , specifically in relation to their native greeting formula, which roughly translates to “who is there”
